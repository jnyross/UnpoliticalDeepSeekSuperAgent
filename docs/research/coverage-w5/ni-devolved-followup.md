## Positive result: a matched selected component for SNP, Plaid and Alliance

**Resolution Foundation, Lalitha Try, _Catastrophic caps_, 31 January 2024.**

- Official publisher body: https://www.resolutionfoundation.org/publications/catastophic-caps/ (the publisher's actual slug omits the second `r`; guessed correctly spelled URL was 404).
- [Retained HTML](<rf-catastrophic-caps-january2024.html>) and [extracted text](<rf-catastrophic-caps-january2024.txt>); [HTTP metadata and SHA-256](<ni-two-child-metadata.json>). A full analysis body, not an index or snippet.
- Exact amount/period/assessment, [text line 84](<rf-catastrophic-caps-january2024.txt#L84>): “Abolishing the two-child limit would cost the Government £2.5 billion in 2024-25, rising to £3.6 billion in 2024-25 prices if the policy were at full coverage. These costs are low compared to the harm that the policy causes, and scrapping the two-child limit would be one of the most efficient ways to drive down child poverty rates. If abolished today, 490,000 children would be lifted out of poverty.”
- [Lines 149–151](<rf-catastrophic-caps-january2024.txt#L149-L151>) specify full rollout in **2035** and £3.6bn in **2024-25 prices**. Do not present the latter as a five-year total or a nominal 2035 forecast.
- Geographic scope: [line 90](<rf-catastrophic-caps-january2024.txt#L90>) explicitly says “families in Great Britain”. Treat the modelled estimate as GB-scoped. No independently verified NI add-on or complete UK fiscal total is claimed.
- Important uncertainty, [line 202](<rf-catastrophic-caps-january2024.txt#L202>): “A major uncertainty associated with this costing is how many of the families that become newly entitled to UC as a result of abolishing the two-child limit would actually claim UC in practice.”
- £2.5bn is **RF's estimate**, not a party's own costing or endorsement of its full manifesto. It is a fiscal-year 2024–25 flow, not a multiyear total; the line does not state a real-terms conversion for this initial-year number. Keep the initial-year estimate separate from the explicitly constant-price steady-state estimate.
- RF also estimates £3bn for abolishing both the two-child limit and benefit cap ([lines 163–166](<rf-catastrophic-caps-january2024.txt#L163-L166>)). Prefer selecting **only two-child-limit abolition** across the three parties: do not accidentally import the combined amount or assess unrelated benefit reforms.

## NI windfall follow-up: retained related body, still not an exact pair

[Full Fact, 2 July 2024, _Will Labour’s energy plans cost 100,000 Scottish jobs?_](https://fullfact.org/election-2024/labour-energy-plans-100000-scottish-jobs/) was successfully obtained from the publisher: [retained body](<ni-fullfact-windfall-jobs.html>), [text](<ni-fullfact-windfall-jobs.txt>), [metadata](<ni-devolved-request-metadata.json>).

It specifies Labour's 35% to 38% Energy Profits Levy, 78% overall sector rate, extension to end of next parliament rather than 2028–29. It judges the SNP's 100,000 **Scottish** jobs claim misleading in scope because Stifel's model covers direct/indirect **UK North Sea** jobs and lacks the no-tax-rise counterfactual and replacement green jobs. This **does not validate SDLP's £1.2bn revenue estimate**, or prove SDLP adopted every Labour parameter. Retain as a related-policy/claim lead, not an exact SDLP fiscal assessment.
