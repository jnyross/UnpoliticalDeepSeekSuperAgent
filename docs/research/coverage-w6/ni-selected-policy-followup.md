## Positive: UUP unrestricted UK-government two-child-limit abolition

The wave-five note's statement that no UUP two-child promise was found is superseded by a direct page extraction. The retained source uses **`twochild` without a hyphen**, explaining why a narrower keyword scan missed it.

- Official party-authored archived PDF: [UUP manifesto](../../../sources/manifesto-2024-uup.pdf#page=15), **PDF p15 / printed p12**. Archive URL: https://www.manifestos.org.uk/manifestos/2024/uup/manifesto.pdf . The archive is not the current party website.
- Independently re-extracted page: [page-15 text](uup-twochild-pdf-page15.txt#L11-L14). Exact party quote: “Ulster Unionist Party MPs will press the next UK government to prioritise addressing poverty and its devastating effects in their policymaking. This must include the abolition of the Universal Credit twochild limit.” Full retained extraction: [lines 542–545](../../../sources/manifesto-2024-uup.txt#L542-L545).
- Allowed publisher: **Resolution Foundation**, Lalitha Try, *Catastrophic caps*, **31 January 2024**. Publisher URL: https://www.resolutionfoundation.org/publications/catastophic-caps/ . [Raw retained HTML](ni-rf-catastrophic-caps-january2024.html), [full retained text](ni-rf-catastrophic-caps-january2024.txt), [integrity/provenance](ni-existing-source-integrity.json). These are copied from the already verified wave-five publisher response, not a new live request.
- Exact publisher body quote, [line 84](ni-rf-catastrophic-caps-january2024.txt#L84): “Abolishing the two-child limit would cost the Government £2.5 billion in 2024-25, rising to £3.6 billion in 2024-25 prices if the policy were at full coverage. These costs are low compared to the harm that the policy causes, and scrapping the two-child limit would be one of the most efficient ways to drive down child poverty rates. If abolished today, 490,000 children would be lifted out of poverty.”
- **Cash / period:** independent RF estimate **£2.5bn expenditure in FY2024–25**; separate **£3.6bn per year at full coverage in 2035, in FY2024–25 prices**. Not a five-year total; no claim that initial-year £2.5bn is explicitly constant-price in this sentence.
- **Baseline / geography:** unrestricted repeal against retaining the two-child limit; modelled **Great Britain**, not full UK. The body says “families in Great Britain” at [line 90](ni-rf-catastrophic-caps-january2024.txt#L90). No verified NI add-on. Not the separate combined two-child/benefit-cap abolition estimate.
- **Status:** a genuine selected component of UUP's prospective UK-government commitment, paired to RF's assessment of the same unrestricted policy's GB portion. Independent costing, not UUP's own costing and not endorsement of its whole manifesto.
- **Uncertainty:** [line 202](ni-rf-catastrophic-caps-january2024.txt#L202): “A major uncertainty associated with this costing is how many of the families that become newly entitled to UC as a result of abolishing the two-child limit would actually claim UC in practice.”

## Bounded remaining leads and mismatches

### DUP

[Manifesto PDF p20](../../../sources/manifesto-2024-dup.pdf#page=20), [text lines1174–1234](../../../sources/manifesto-2024-dup.txt#L1174-L1234), contains further unspecified NI reductions, opposing income-tax threshold freezes, abolishing domestic electricity VAT and maintaining the fuel-duty freeze. The generic employee-NI reduction does not state a rate or date, so a 2p-cut model cannot simply be substituted. The electricity-only VAT pledge is not a domestic-fuel VAT repeal or a one-year emergency reduction. [Lines1255–1256](../../../sources/manifesto-2024-dup.txt#L1255-L1256) restrict UC repeal to **3- and4-child households**, unlike RF unrestricted repeal. No exact allowed-body cost/assessment obtained for these designs.

A potentially useful **different** offer is “maintain the freeze on fuel duty” (line1234), but duration and treatment of the temporary5p cut are not separately specified. Ordinary OBR public HTML requests succeeded through `web_fetch`, but raw-retention requests returned403 and the normal linked March2024 fuel-duty PDF download also returned403: [request metadata](ni-followup-metadata.json), [PDF request](ni-obr-fuel-pdf-request.json). No bypass was attempted. The fetched current OBR explainer (https://obr.uk/forecasts-in-depth/tax-by-tax-spend-by-spend/fuel-duties/) explicitly uses **November2025**, not election2024, baseline assumptions; its £3.6bn average lower receipts over2027–28 to2030–31 if duty stays constant is **not** offered as a 2024 DUP cost. A contemporaneous retained baseline/design match is still needed.

[PDF p21](../../../sources/manifesto-2024-dup.pdf#page=21), [lines1275–1288](../../../sources/manifesto-2024-dup.txt#L1275-L1288), records an **already announced** £25m NI childcare package (sector stabilisation,22.5 hours preschool, subsidy scheme). This is not an independently-costed new Westminster election pledge; no implementation-period total inferred. Earlier monetary achievements also are not forward costs.

### Sinn Féin

The complete retained2024 Westminster PDF has only9 numbered content pages. The public-services commitment at [PDF p7 / printed p7](../../../sources/manifesto-2024-sinnfein.pdf#page=7), [text lines274–288](../../../sources/manifesto-2024-sinnfein.txt#L274-L288), records **£688m already allocated** for public-sector pay and seeks future fiscal powers, including progressive taxation and minimum-wage powers, without rates or prospective cash cost. A keyword check of this specific retained text found no water-charge design or tax-benefit rates that can be paired to a quantified allowed-body assessment. That is a bounded document result, not a universal absence claim. Republic-of-Ireland budget/election costings surfaced by search were not substituted for this UK-election offer.

### SDLP

The retained [text lines425–430](../../../sources/manifesto-2024-sdlp.txt#L425-L430) proposes Executive regional-rate revenue to fund ending the two-child policy: NI mitigation, not a UK/GB-wide repeal commitment in this passage. No transfer of RF's GB £2.5bn to that NI scheme. Its [windfall quote lines253–256](../../../sources/manifesto-2024-sdlp.txt#L253-L256), projected **annual average £1.2bn**, leaves rate, allowances and averaging window unstated. No newly obtained allowed-body body closes that exact-design gap.
