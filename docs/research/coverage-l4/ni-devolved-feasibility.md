Research checked 2026-11-02 UTC. **Initial research audit, not Coverage L4 evidence or a completed 13-party comparison.** The owner subsequently directed partial implementation on issue #32: publish the verified Conservative/Labour/Lib Dem evidence and keep other gaps explicit, without claiming L4 or closing the issue. It concentrates on the six Northern Ireland parties and Plaid Cymru/SNP; the five other rows below are leads, not an assertion that their complete evidence packages have been assembled.

## Conclusion

A useful attributed comparison is feasible in part. Complete 13-party coverage is **not yet evidenced**, even if the requirement permits selected costed proposals rather than a whole-manifesto assessment. Concrete unresolved prerequisites include a forward-looking quantified Sinn Féin Westminster proposal with a matching allowed-body assessment, and a matching assessment of UUP's minimum £35m/year Skills Fund. These are bounded gaps in the material checked, **not proof that evidence does not exist anywhere**. Do not invent costings, assign another party's totals to a party, or treat an unrelated fact-check as fiscal-plan analysis.

Only IFS, OBR, Resolution Foundation, Full Fact and NAO may supply judgements. Party documents establish what parties propose, not credibility. A critical assessment is valid evidence; an endorsement is not required. Nor does every row necessarily need a complete fiscal scorecard: a selected costed policy can be a legitimate scoped candidate if party commitment, amount, period, geographical coverage and independent analysis actually match.

- [Full Fact buyback-tax analysis, 6 June 2024](https://fullfact.org/politics/would-the-lib-dems-plan-to-tax-share-buybacks-raise-14-billion-a-year/), [retained text](<fullfact-buybacks.txt>): Lib Dem 4% tax on the 100 biggest stock-exchange corporations, claimed £1.4bn/year after behavioural adjustment. IFS economist Stuart Adam told Full Fact he would be surprised if a 4% UK buyback tax raised much revenue because firms could switch to dividends; he described it as distortionary. This is direct allowed-body assessment of a **Lib Dem proposal**, and related-mechanism evidence for Alliance, whose design is unspecified in the checked passage.
- [Full Fact Reform check](https://fullfact.org/election-2024/reformuk-manifesto/), [retained text](<fullfact-reform.txt>): waiting lists, immigration claims, GDP, policing, crime and postal voting. Not evidence of the cost/credibility of the economic promises quoted by TUV.

Discusses Conservative £17bn tax cuts versus £23bn pre-announced rises, difficulty delivering £12bn welfare savings, avoidance assumptions, and missing departmental allocations. Genuine fiscal-plan judgement for Conservative/Labour, not transferable to other parties.
- [OBR FAQ](https://obr.uk/faqs/), [retained text](<obr-faq.txt>): under “Are you allowed to scrutinise and certify opposition party manifesto policies?” answers **No**, citing its statutory restriction to current government policies. Also distinguishes its role from NAO's generally retrospective spending/value-for-money role. This rules out assuming OBR supplies missing opposition-manifesto certification; it does not rule out relevant OBR costing evidence for an already implemented matching policy.

Search identified publisher IFS responses for [SNP](https://ifs.org.uk/articles/initial-response-snps-manifesto) and [Plaid Cymru](https://ifs.org.uk/articles/plaid-cymru-manifesto-response). ## Cross-policy alternatives and safeguards

1. **Alliance/Lib Dem buybacks:** there is actual relevant allowed-body analysis, not a blank row. Publish only as clearly scoped related-design evidence unless Alliance's rate/scope can be established. The same £1.4bn amount alone does not prove identical design.
2. **TUV/Reform:** explicit manifesto quotation supports a better policy-identity chain than merely noticing similar slogans. Still retain the exact Reform costing and analysis of the adopted subsection; never import an unadopted total.
3. **SDLP/Labour windfall levy:** £1.2bn annual-average estimate offers a concrete path. Need exact rate/base/allowance/timing checks before transferring an assessment.
4. **DUP/two-child cap:** restricted to households with three/four children, unlike unrestricted abolition. Plaid/SNP may support full abolition, but this audit did not retain exact commitment plus official costing plus matching assessment for that route. Sinn Féin's checked Westminster document does not contain that proposal.
5. A missing overall cost schedule is not by itself disqualifying if selected costed proposals satisfy the actual acceptance test. Conversely, policy percentages, retrospective achievements, existing allocations and cost-of-living savings to households are not automatically public-expenditure costings.
