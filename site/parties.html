Party offers: a 2024 snapshot

These are past plans, not today's plans. The list of Commons parties is from 2024-07-09. Each offer comes from a party's 2024 general election manifesto: its book of plans. A linked party release may add the cash claim. We do not say these plans are now law. We do not say they have funds or are still party policy.

We show one tax, benefit or public funding offer per party. Each has the same fields and word limits. Parties are in name order. These are examples, not the full plans. We do not rank them. We use no party colours or scores.

How to read this page

Data period: 2024-07-09. Snapshot as of 2024-07-09. Publish and update dates not supplied.

This is a fixed past snapshot. No next release date is announced.

The offer sums up a party claim. The quote is the party's own text, not our view. Costs are party claims or an outside estimate. We label which. They are not our own estimates. An outside quote gives the named source’s view, not ours. It may cover a wider plan; we say so in Scope. We do not rate plans. A missing cost is not zero. “Unavailable here” means this set of data does not give it for this offer. It may exist elsewhere.

The rule is the same for all parties. We pick one direct tax, benefit or public funding pledge. We add a short quote from the source. We add a cash claim where we have checked the party source. We may use a named body's cost for a matching policy. That is not a party costing or a full plan review. Its place, year and limits are shown. We add a short outside quote where it fits. We use only IFS, OBR, Resolution Foundation, Full Fact or NAO. Our choice does not show which policy matters most to a party. Each source is a manifesto from that party. Some saved PDFs come from the British Manifesto Archive, not the party's own site. We label these copies. Where we have not checked a full date, we give just the year. PDF page counts start at the first page, with the cover. We keep the quote's words. We remove line breaks.

Each field has a word limit. The offer can use up to 45 words. The quote can use up to 85. Scope and baseline can each use 30. Year and status can each use 25. Costing and its quote can use 30 in all. Assessment and its named source can use 30 in all. Party names and source page labels can each use 15 words. Source titles can use 35 words. Host labels can use 30. Source web links can use 2,000 characters. The checks use these limits for each party.

End the two-child limit on benefits.
Party source excerpt
A wealth of evidence has demonstrated the link between the two-child limit in Universal Credit and higher poverty rates among larger families, with a harsh impact on children. Scrapping the policy would be one of the most efficient ways of reducing child poverty across the UK.

Selected two-child policy only. Resolution Foundation models Great Britain, not a full UK or party plan cost; Northern Ireland, the benefit cap and bedroom tax excluded.
Baseline
RF compares abolition with keeping the limit. Benefit take-up may differ in practice. Other benefit changes are excluded.

RF estimate for 2024-25; cash, not a lifetime total. Research published before the election.
Status
2024 election proposal; not current spending or a claim of policy now in force.
Costing

Resolution Foundation: one of the most efficient ways to drive down child poverty rates

Checks open the saved Commons list and each PDF. They check that we cover all parties on the list. They check each quote is on the page we cite. They check that file hashes match. A hash is a code used to check a file has not changed. They also check the same fields, order and length limits for all parties. If a source changes, we stop the update for review. We also check new cash quotes on the saved PDF page or web text block. We read saved web pages again to check outside quotes. Some rows still lack a cost or a matched outside view. We name those gaps; they are not proof that no such source exists. Get the checked claims, source details and gaps as data. The same evidence rule applies to all.
