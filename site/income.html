UK tax, receipts, borrowing and debt

In 2025-26, the UK public sector took in £1,231.3 billion. Of this, £1,104.7 billion was tax and £126.6 billion was non-tax income. These are outturn figures: recorded results that can still change. They are not forecasts.

Spending was £1,365.6 billion. The gap was £134.3 billion of net borrowing. Debt stood at £2,916.8 billion at end-March 2026.

VAT has two rows in this source. Its national accounts tax total includes the VAT refund row. Do not take refunds off a second time. A minus sign means net repayments or a tax adjustment, not a missing figure.

The source groups some taxes with other receipts. “Other taxes” is the tax total less all the named tax rows. It is not a claim to know each tax in that group. This avoids calling a mixed tax and non-tax line all tax.

Non-tax income was £126.6 billion. It includes interest, dividends and income from trade by public bodies. The source calls one item gross operating surplus. This means trading income before the cost of worn-out assets, under national accounts rules. It is not all cash paid in by households.

We find non-tax receipts by taking tax off total receipts. Other non-tax receipts are what is left after interest, dividends and gross operating surplus. This is what is left over, not a new named source of income.

Borrowing is not tax. Debt is a stock.

Data period: 2025-26; debt at end-March 2026. Published 2026-10-28. Source updated 2026-10-28.

No next release date is announced on the retained OBR data page. Databanks are normally updated around two working days after the monthly public finance release. Official source. Saved source page (as of 2026-11-01).

Borrowing is the gap between spending and receipts in a year. It is not a tax or a receipt. Net borrowing uses the accrued basis. This means income and costs count when they arise, not just when cash moves.

Debt is a stock at a point in time. Net debt is debts owed less liquid assets. These are assets that are cash or can soon turn into cash. It is not the same as all assets less all debts. Do not add debt to annual income. A change in debt need not equal that year’s borrowing: loans, asset sales and the timing of cash can also change debt.

On this source basis, receipts plus net borrowing equal Total Managed Expenditure (TME). TME is not the same as spending on services. We do not mix these OBR sums with a separate Treasury release. See the spending page and its own scope.

The tables give exact sheet names and cell codes. The breakdown tables use 2025-26 outturn. The summary also shows every forecast year in the workbook, from 2026-27 to 2031-32. Each debt row is a stock at year end. We select the latest complete outturn before the first forecast year in the source note; forecasts retain that note’s own vintage.

Raw values are retained to 12 decimal places in GBP billion; HTML rounds to 0.1 billion. Source actuals are recorded to 0.001 billion. Reconciliations use unrounded data with 0.000001 billion numeric tolerance, not independently rounded display amounts.

All identities use one OBR workbook vintage and public sector accounting basis. Tax rows C:AC plus the derived other-tax residual equal national accounts taxes. AF is a mixed tax/non-tax line, not a tax type. It is split by residual against the published tax total; the residuals do not identify further individual taxes. The VAT refund row belongs in the published national accounts tax basis: do not subtract it again or treat it as non-tax. Negative tax entries are net receipts or repayments, not missing data. TME less current receipts equals net borrowing on this accrued basis. Debt is not added to annual receipts or borrowing; changes in debt also reflect financial transactions and cash/accrual differences. This workbook is not reconciled to a separate Treasury workbook vintage.

Checks on the source basis

  • Tax breakdown: sum(tax-type records) - taxes = 0 £bn (tolerance 1e-06 £bn).
  • Non-tax breakdown: sum(non-tax-type records) - non-tax = -0 £bn (tolerance 1e-06 £bn).
  • Mixed other line split: other-taxes + other-non-tax - mixed-other-receipts = -0 £bn (tolerance 1e-06 £bn).
  • Spending and receipts: tme - receipts - borrowing = 0 £bn (tolerance 1e-06 £bn).
  • Receipts across sheets: Receipts AG - Aggregates C = 0 £bn (tolerance 1e-06 £bn).
  • Taxes across sheets: Receipts AH - Aggregates I = 0 £bn (tolerance 1e-06 £bn).

Source notes and definitions

  • Outturn fiscal data consistent with the ONS/HM Treasury Public Sector Finances Statistical Bulletin released on 22 September 2026. (Receipts (£bn)!C40)
  • Forecast years from 2026-27 are consistent with the OBR Economic and fiscal outlook forecast published October 2026. (Receipts (£bn)!C41)
  • 1 Includes road lorry user charge. 2 Also includes capacity markets, green gas levy and warm home discount. 3 Also includes, residential property developers tax and Pillar 2 taxes (Receipts (£bn)!C42)
  • Revenue relating to activities in the current year, comprising mainly direct and indirect taxes, but also including social security contributions, interest, dividends, capital taxes and profits from trading activities. (Glossary!C5)
  • The sum of public sector current expenditure, public sector net investment and public sector depreciation. (Glossary!C6)
  • The difference between total public sector receipts and expenditure on an accrued basis each year. As the widest measure of borrowing it is a key indicator of the fiscal position. PSNB is the headline measure of 'the deficit'. (Glossary!C11)
  • A stock measure of the public sector's net liability position i.e. its liabilities minus its liquid assets. It is broadly the stock equivalent of public sector net borrowing, but measured on a cash rather than an accrued basis. It is also the fiscal measure used for the Government's supplementary fiscal target. PSND is the key measure of the country's overall debt. (Glossary!C14)
  • A measure of the amount of cash which is needed to make up the difference betweeen spending and revenues. This is different from net borrowing as it also includes financial transactions, which will affect the cash position but are not included in net borrowing. Public sector net debt is driven by changes in the public sector net cash requirement. (Glossary!C15)
